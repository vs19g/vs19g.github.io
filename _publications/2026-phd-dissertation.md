My Ph.D. dissertation in Physics at Florida State University, defended on November 3, 2026. The full text is under a one-year embargo and will be available through FSU once it ends.

### Abstract
$$\alpha$$-particle-induced reactions mediate the synthesis and redistribution of nuclear material in explosive stellar environments such as Type I X-ray bursts and core-collapse supernovae, yet directly measured cross sections for most astrophysically relevant $$(\alpha,p)$$ reactions remain sparse. This dissertation reports the upgrade of the ANASEN active-target detector at the John D. Fox Superconducting Linear Accelerator Laboratory and its first in-beam measurements: $$^{27}\mathrm{Al}(\alpha,p)^{30}\mathrm{Si}$$ with a stable beam, the primary dataset analyzed, and $$^{17}\mathrm{F}(\alpha,p)^{20}\mathrm{Ne}$$, a breakout path from the hot CNO cycle, with a radioactive beam.

The upgrade replaced the ASIC-based readout with a digital data-acquisition system and the proportional counter with [TWINE](/portfolio/twine-shockley-ramo/), whose counter-twisted anode and cathode wires give the longitudinal position through their crossings and the cathode charge fraction. The detector was calibrated with $$\alpha$$ sources, pulser runs, and elastic scattering, and the position reconstruction of each event topology was benchmarked against the anode–cathode crossings.

With the $$^{27}\mathrm{Al}$$ beam, protons from $$^{27}\mathrm{Al}(\alpha,p)^{30}\mathrm{Si}$$ were reconstructed in every event topology, more than a million of them in the downstream silicon barrel. The excitation-energy spectrum does not resolve the individual states of $$^{30}\mathrm{Si}$$, and the reconstructed yield far exceeds Hauser–Feshbach predictions without the expected drop-off at the lowest energies. The difference is attributed either to stopping powers for the intense beam far below those established with $$\alpha$$ particles or to events other than reaction protons, so the data do not yet constrain the cross section. The $$^{17}\mathrm{F}$$ data share these limitations with far fewer events and a larger beam spot, and are not analyzed in the dissertation.

The campaign was limited chiefly by two conditions: an error in the surveyed chamber geometry placed the astrophysical window upstream of the analyzed detectors, and the proportional-counter thresholds did not reach the sensitivity required for protons. Simulations show that a beam entering the gas at 80 MeV places the astrophysical window over the proportional counter, and that a lower threshold separates the ground and first excited states of $$^{30}\mathrm{Si}$$. These corrections define the next measurement with the upgraded detector.
