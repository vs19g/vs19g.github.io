Before moving into nuclear physics instrumentation, I worked on a project at **BITS Pilani - Hyderabad** (Jan–May 2018, advised by Prof. Kannan Ramaswamy and Dr. Vikranth K. Surasani) combining my dual background in Physics and Chemical Engineering: simulating a **Nano-Photonics Enabled Solar Membrane Distillation (NESMD)** plant using a bilayer membrane architecture.

### The Idea
Membrane distillation drives water vapor across a hydrophobic membrane using a temperature gradient. Coupling this with nanophotonic solar absorption lets the temperature gradient be generated directly by sunlight at the membrane surface, rather than by external heating, making the process viable off-grid.

### My Work
* **Process Simulation:** Simulated the NESMD plant's bilayer membrane architecture to model the coupled optical absorption and vapor transport behavior.
* **Site Viability:** Evaluated the geographic viability of NESMD for Hyderabad, India, optimizing system parameters to guide the design of a prototype desalination plant.

I presented this work as *"Desalination of Water using Nanophotonics"* at the National Symposium on Emerging Environmental Challenges: An Engineering Approach, Hyderabad, in 2018.

### Why It Still Matters to Me
This project was my first exposure to carrying a design from first-principles modeling through to a proposed physical prototype — the same design-simulate-validate loop I now apply to detector instrumentation at the John D. Fox Laboratory, just applied to a very different length scale and physics.
